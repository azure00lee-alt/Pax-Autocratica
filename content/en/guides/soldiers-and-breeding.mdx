## The workforce loop

### Prerequisites before expanding the workforce

Official material connects colony performance to food, healthcare, comfort, policy, surveillance, punishment, propaganda, and feasts. Before relying on a new generation of soldiers, make sure the current population can eat, recover, and spend time away from work. This is a **practical inference**, not a published formula: a larger population only helps when the colony can sustain it.

Use this preparation check:

1. Confirm food production and access are stable.
2. Keep healthcare and basic comfort available.
3. Leave enough staffing slack for `Time Off` rather than assigning every adult continuously.
4. Review the four immediate condition readouts before changing policy or adding duties.

### A practical workforce and breeding workflow

The official sequence that can be verified is social interaction, a relationship outcome, pregnancy with an expected delivery date, growth to adulthood at age 18, and formal enlistment at Victory Square. A safe management workflow is therefore:

1. Stabilize the existing soldiers' conditions and work schedule.
2. Allow social interactions to occur; do not treat pregnancy as an instant recruitment button.
3. Track the expected delivery date shown by the interface.
4. Plan food, space, and future assignments during childhood.
5. At age 18, send the new adult through formal enlistment at Victory Square.
6. Assign work by relevant attributes, then re-check conditions after the schedule settles.

## Soldier roles and life stages

### What can be confirmed about soldier types

The approved public sources describe soldiers as a managed workforce with social interactions, relationships, work attributes, age, and enlistment. They do **not** provide a stable named taxonomy detailed enough for a reliable class-by-class encyclopedia. This page therefore organizes soldiers by life stage and assignment readiness instead of inventing class names.

<Callout type="source" title="Confidence: limited taxonomy">
  Verified: soldiers have attributes and can be assigned to work. Not verified from the approved sources: a complete list of named soldier classes, class icons, or fixed class bonuses.
</Callout>

### Life-stage management

- **Child:** not yet eligible for the adult enlistment step. Plan future capacity rather than counting this soldier as present labor.
- **Adult:** begins at age 18 and can proceed to Victory Square for formal enlistment.
- **Aged:** official update language states that the Aged condition reduces movement speed and work attributes. Move these soldiers away from assignments where reduced speed or attributes create a bottleneck.

The last recommendation is a practical inference. The verified part is the penalty; the best reassignment depends on the jobs and shortages in your own colony.

## The four core states

### Read the condition panel before changing assignments

Official source language presents four immediate soldier condition readouts: Wellbeing, Hunger, Exhaustion, and Fear. They should be read together rather than optimized in isolation. Colony-wide policy language may also discuss broader happiness or loyalty outcomes; those are not treated here as replacements for the four immediate readouts.

| Readout | What it signals | First management check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wellbeing | Overall comfort and health pressure | Check healthcare, comfort, food access, and whether the schedule allows recovery. |
| Hunger | Whether food needs are being met | Confirm both supply and practical access before expanding the workforce. |
| Exhaustion | Whether duty is outpacing recovery | Put the soldier on `Time Off`, then confirm the state actually improves. |
| Fear | Pressure created by colony conditions and control | Review policy, surveillance, punishment, propaganda, feasts, and recent changes together. |

### Practical management order

When several readouts look bad, start with survival and recovery: Hunger, then Exhaustion, then Wellbeing. Review Fear alongside the policy or enforcement change that preceded it. This order is a **practical inference**, not an official priority formula; an urgent colony event may justify a different order.

Avoid changing several systems at once. Make one food, schedule, comfort, or policy adjustment, allow time for the interface to update, and then compare the four readouts again. That makes the cause of improvement or decline easier to identify.

## Relationships, pregnancy and adulthood

### Breeding requirements and sequence

Verified official material supports this sequence: soldiers socialize, relationships can result, pregnancy can occur, and the interface shows an expected delivery date. The public sources do not publish a guaranteed pairing recipe, exact probability, or universal minimum-stat threshold. Treat stable living conditions and schedule slack as sensible preparation, not as a hidden formula.

### What to monitor during pregnancy and childhood

- Record the expected delivery date from the interface.
- Keep food and recovery capacity above the needs of the current workforce.
- Do not count a child as an immediately assignable adult.
- Prepare a future job before age 18, but wait for the formal adult enlistment step.

The first and third points are direct consequences of official information. The capacity planning points are practical inferences intended to prevent a population increase from creating a new shortage.

## Assignment and enlistment

### Manual and automatic assignment

Official update language says auto-assignment prioritizes soldiers with stronger attributes relevant to the work. Use it as a starting point, then inspect whether the result leaves enough recovery time and whether an Aged soldier's penalties make another assignment more suitable.

<Callout type="info" title="Practical inference">
  The strongest attribute match is not always the best colony-wide choice. A critical worker who is hungry or exhausted may produce a better result after recovery than after another immediate assignment.
</Callout>

### Enlistment checklist at age 18

1. Confirm the soldier has reached adulthood at age 18.
2. Send the adult to Victory Square for formal enlistment.
3. Compare relevant work attributes before choosing a duty.
4. Use auto-assignment when appropriate, then review the actual distribution.
5. Re-check Wellbeing, Hunger, Exhaustion, and Fear after the new schedule begins.

Steps one through three reflect verified official information. Steps four and five are a practical verification loop.

## Common problems

### Symptom-to-check troubleshooting

- **Exhaustion does not recover:** verify that the soldier is actually in the `Time Off` recovery state. If the assignment keeps returning, temporarily remove or adjust the duty and observe one change at a time.
- **A new adult is unavailable for work:** confirm age 18 has been reached and complete formal enlistment at Victory Square.
- **Auto-assignment produces a poor roster:** compare the relevant work attributes, then check whether condition or age penalties make the nominally strongest worker a bad practical fit.
- **Pregnancy seems stalled:** use the expected delivery date displayed by the interface instead of estimating from real time. Leave schedule and resource capacity available while waiting.
- **Conditions worsen after a policy change:** compare all four immediate readouts and reverse or isolate one recent change before making another.

Only the `Time Off`, age-18, Victory Square, expected-date, and attribute-priority behaviors are directly supported by the approved official material. The isolation steps are troubleshooting practice, not confirmed game rules.

**Before reporting a bug**

- Save the current four readouts and assignment.
- Note age, life stage, and whether `Time Off` is active.
- Record the expected delivery date if pregnancy is involved.
- Reproduce the issue after changing only one assignment or policy.
- Check the official Steam announcements for a documented change.
